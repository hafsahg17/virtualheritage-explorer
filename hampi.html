Welcome to Hampi

Hampi is a historic village located on the banks of the Tungabhadra River in Karnataka. It was once the capital of the powerful Vijayanagara Empire and became one of the most prosperous cities in medieval India.

Today, Hampi is famous for its spectacular stone temples, royal structures, massive boulders, ancient markets and remarkable architectural remains. Its monuments tell the story of a rich cultural and artistic past.

A Glorious Past

History of Hampi

Hampi flourished as the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, which was founded in 1336 by Harihara I and Bukka Raya I. Under rulers such as Krishnadevaraya, the city developed into an important centre of trade, religion, art and culture.

Traders from different parts of the world visited Vijayanagara, bringing wealth and cultural exchange. The city was known for its grand temples, busy markets, royal buildings and advanced water-management systems.

Following the Battle of Talikota in 1565, the city suffered extensive destruction and gradually declined. Its surviving monuments now provide valuable evidence of the empire's achievements.

Architectural Highlights

Hampi showcases a remarkable combination of Dravidian temple architecture, detailed stone carvings and impressive royal structures.

Stone Chariot

Located in the Vittala Temple complex, the iconic stone chariot is one of Hampi's most recognised architectural masterpieces.

Virupaksha Temple

A historic temple dedicated to Lord Shiva, known for its towering entrance gopuram, beautiful carvings and active religious traditions.

Lotus Mahal

A beautiful two-storeyed royal structure recognised for its lotus-inspired arches and elegant blend of architectural styles.

Vittala Temple

Famous for its elaborate pillars, grand halls and the celebrated stone chariot in its courtyard.

Royal Enclosure

An important area containing remains of royal platforms, audience halls and ancient water systems.

Ancient Market Streets

Long rows of stone-pillared pavilions reveal how trade and public life once flourished in Hampi.
